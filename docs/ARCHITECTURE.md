# Architecture

This describes the system as it stands now. The reasoning behind each choice, including the ones that were reversed along the way, is in [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md).

## 1. Goals and non-goals

**Goals**

- A provider assembles an order for a patient and sets the patient-facing price per item.
- The patient pays through a stubbed payment step.
- Every paid order is split into wholesale cost (COGS), provider margin and a 75 bps platform fee. The three parts sum exactly to the amount paid.
- A paid order is auditable from stored data alone: every cent is attributed to one of those three buckets in an append-only double-entry ledger (revision 16).
- Providers can find any of their orders by status, and a dashboard shows sales totals and current stock. A payment attempt reserves stock before charging, a decline gives it back, and a recorded payment keeps it as the sale. An admin (revision 13) can add to or set a product's available stock; providers and patients can't.
- GMV and platform fee revenue are directly queryable.

**Non-goals** (from the brief)

- Real payments, auth, email, shipping or fulfillment.
- Tax, shipping cost, regulatory compliance, multi-state or international handling.
- Warehouse or multi-location inventory.
- Refunds. Only orders awaiting payment can be cancelled; paid orders have no actions.

## 2. Principles

1. **The money path comes first.** The split must be correct, deterministic and verifiable from stored rows.
2. **Clean seams for everything external.** Payments, auth and notifications are small interfaces with fake implementations, injected at startup.
3. **One home for the money rules.** The split, fee, minimum price and markup conversions live in `@supplement-orders/domain`, and both the API and the browser call the same functions.
4. **Smallest thing that proves the flow.** One API, one SPA, one database.

Order status lifecycle:

```
            ┌──────────────────── reopen ───────────────────┐
            v                                               │
 (new) ──> draft ──send──> pending_payment ──pay──> paid     │
            │                    │                          │
          delete               cancel ──> cancelled ────────┘
            v
        (row removed)
```

- **Delete** removes a draft and its lines for good. A draft has no `sentAt`, so it has no payment, no journal, and the patient can't see it. That's why a hard delete is safe. A draft created by reopen was sent earlier, but reopen already cleared that history. If that draft is an original whose back-orders were cancelled with it, they are deleted with it in the same transaction (revision 15.3); they were never paid, so no money records are involved.
- **Cancel** applies only to `pending_payment`. Drafts are deleted instead, and paid orders can't be cancelled. Cancelling an order also cancels every downstream back-order in its chain (revision 15.3).
- **Reopen** moves `cancelled` back to `draft`. It clears `sentAt`, `cancelledAt` and the stored totals, keeps the lines, and hides the order from the patient until it's sent again. A back-order is never reopened (409 `BACKORDER_NOT_EDITABLE`). Revision 18.1: reopening an original merges the lines of its cancelled downstream back-orders back into the draft (a product already on it gets the quantities added at its own price; any other comes back at the back-order line's price and snapshots), so the provider gets the whole original order back. Each merged back-order is stamped `merged_back_at`, so it's merged only once, and otherwise stays cancelled history. A resent order can split again, because only a live back-order counts.
- **Awaiting stock** (revision 14) is not a stored status. The view `orders_with_status` (migration `0012`, recreated by `0013`) reports `effective_status = 'awaiting_stock'` for a `pending_payment` order none of whose lines has a product with `stock_quantity > 0`, and the stored status otherwise. Every order read (detail, lists, status filters, a patient's latest status) selects from the view; every write and row lock targets `orders`. Nothing updates orders when stock changes, so a restock or a payment is reflected on the next read. An order whose own unresolved payment attempt holds its stock is never awaiting stock (revision 15.1): the reservation is its stock, even when it took the last units.
- **Back-order chains pay in order** (revision 15.3). A back-order can't be paid while the order it was split from is unpaid: pay answers 409 `PRIOR_ORDER_UNPAID` with `error.priorOrderId`, whatever the stock, and the order DTOs carry `blockedByOrderId`. An attempt already in progress on a back-order is still completed.
- **Paid orders are frozen** (revision 15.5, migration `0013`). Row triggers reject any change to a paid order except `updated_at`, deleting it, and any insert, update or delete of its lines (SQLSTATE `23000`).

Every state change (edit, send, delete, cancel, reopen) runs in a transaction that first locks the order row with `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`, so any two of them on the same order serialize: the second waits on the row lock. If the first left a status the second action doesn't allow, the second gets a 409, or a 404 if the row was deleted. Two edits of an order that is still a draft both succeed, and the later one wins. Pay is the exception: it's several short transactions, each taking the same lock, with the gateway call in between and outside any lock (§6). An unresolved payment attempt is what keeps the other actions out while the charge is in flight. A second pay of an order the first pay already completed returns the existing result, not a 409. None of the actions except pay touches stock or the ledger.

Why a ledger rather than three columns on the order: "where did every cent go" becomes a query over append-only rows, and future adjustments (refunds, corrections, payouts) can be new journals instead of mutations. Why double entry (revision 16) rather than the earlier three typed rows per payment: every movement now names both sides, so the books prove themselves (total debits equal total credits in the trial balance), what the platform owes each provider is an account balance rather than a derived sum, and a payout or refund is just another balanced journal against the same accounts.

Database constraints back the model:

- Every money column is `bigint` cents. `products.cogs_cents`, `order_items.unit_price_cents`, `order_items.unit_cogs_cents`, `payment_attempts.amount_cents`, and `payments.amount_cents` have a `>= 0` check. `postings.amount_cents` is signed and checked non-zero at commit. The three order totals (`subtotal_cents`, `platform_fee_cents`, `total_cents`) don't; their non-negativity is an application invariant.
- `orders_total_is_subtotal`: the patient total equals the subtotal. This constraint was added `NOT VALID` so rows from before that rule are left alone; every new or updated row is checked.
- `orders_fee_within_subtotal`, `orders_paid_at_iff_paid`, `orders_cancelled_at_iff_cancelled`, `orders_sent_unless_draft_or_cancelled`, `orders_totals_present_once_sent`, and `orders_revision_counts_sends` (a sent order has a revision of at least 1). (`cancelled` without `sentAt` is still allowed because revision 6 let drafts be cancelled; new cancels always have `sentAt`.)
- `order_items`: quantity `>= 1`, one line per product per order, and a cost, name and SKU snapshot exactly when a product is set.
- Unique `payments.order_id`, unique `payments.idempotency_key`, unique `payments.payment_attempt_id`, unique `journals.payment_id`, unique `postings(journal_id, account_id)`, unique `accounts.code` and `accounts.provider_id`.
- `payment_attempts`: unique `idempotency_key`, unique `(order_id, revision, execution)`, `execution >= 1`, and a gateway reference exactly when the status is `succeeded`, `late_success` or `refund_required` (revision 19.1); `refunded_at` only on `refund_required`. Since migration `0015` (revision 17.3) the database requires every payment to name a `succeeded` attempt on the same order, and since `0017` (revision 19.2) the payment's amount, idempotency key and gateway reference must equal the attempt's and the attempt's revision the order's. Since `0016` (revision 18.4) an attempt a payment references is frozen: a trigger rejects any update, even of `updated_at`, and any delete.
- `orders_split_from_order_id_live_unique` (revision 18.1, `0016`): a partial unique index on `split_from_order_id` where the status isn't `cancelled`, so an order has at most one live back-order.
- `installation` (revision 18.4, `0016`): a single row holding this database's `install_id` UUID, the prefix of every new idempotency key.

The ledger is also enforced by the database itself, not only by the application. Revision 11b (migration `0010`) introduced this for `ledger_entries`; revision 16 (migration `0014`) replaced that table with journals and postings and moved the guards over:

- **Append-only:** `BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE` row triggers on `payments`, `journals` and `postings` reject any change with an integrity error (SQLSTATE `23000`). On `accounts` the only change allowed is a rename (a provider's payable follows its user's name). The application has no update or delete path for any of them.
- **Balanced at commit:** a `DEFERRABLE INITIALLY DEFERRED` constraint trigger on `journals` and `postings` requires every journal to have at least two postings, none of them 0, summing exactly to 0.
- **Payment linkage at commit:** a deferred trigger on `payments`, `journals` and `postings` requires every payment to have exactly one `patient_payment` journal on the payment's order, with a Cash debit of exactly `payments.amount_cents` and credits (negative amounts) only to Product sales, Platform fee revenue and the payable of that order's provider. With the balance check, the credits sum to the payment. A payment without its journal fails at commit, and since zero postings are omitted, a payment of 0 can never commit.
- **Split and provenance at commit** (revision 17.3, migration `0015`): the same deferred trigger, now calling `assert_payment_journal(payment_id)`, also requires the Product sales credit to equal the sum of the order's `quantity × unit_cogs_cents` snapshots, the Platform fee revenue credit to equal the order's stored `platform_fee_cents` (every sent order stores it, so there is no fallback to the fee formula), and the provider payable credit to equal the payment less both, each omitted when 0. The payment's `payment_attempt_id` must reference a `succeeded` attempt for the same order. Migration `0017` (revision 19.2) adds `assert_payment_provenance(payment_id)` to the same trigger: the payment's `amount_cents`, `idempotency_key` and `gateway_reference` must equal its attempt's, and the attempt's `revision` the paid order's, so a forged attempt for 1¢ can't back a 5,000¢ payment. So a balanced journal with the wrong split, such as every dollar after cost booked as fee, fails at commit. The migration runs the check over every existing payment and fails, rolling back, if any row can't pass; it never loosens the check.
- **Payable accounts:** an `AFTER INSERT` trigger on `providers` creates `2100-<providerId>` Payable to the provider's name in the same statement, whatever the create path.
- **If the application ever trips a trigger**, that is a bug: the pay transaction rolls back and the request returns 500.
- **Resets** in tests, e2e and `db:seed` use `TRUNCATE`, which doesn't fire row triggers, and then restore the system accounts so their ids stay 1-3. Production data is never reset.

Revision 15.5 (migration `0013`) freezes paid orders the same way: a `BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE` trigger on `orders` rejects any change to a paid row except `updated_at`, and any delete; a trigger on `order_items` rejects inserting, updating or deleting a line of a paid order. The paid split the provider sees (cost of goods, margin, fee) is read from the journal's postings, never recomputed from the lines. Revision 18.4 (migration `0016`) freezes the payment attempt a payment references the same way, more strictly: not even `updated_at` may change.

## 5. Money model

All amounts are integer cents in USD. The functions below are in `packages/domain/src/money/`.

| Rule              | Definition                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Patient total     | `total = subtotal = sum(quantity * unitPriceCents)`. The patient pays exactly what the provider priced                                          |
| Processing fee    | `computePlatformFee(subtotal) = floor((subtotal * 75 + 9999) / 10000)`: 75 bps of the subtotal, rounded up to the cent, once per order          |
| Provider margin   | `subtotal - cogs - fee`                                                                                                                         |
| Minimum price     | `minUnitPriceCents(cost)`: the smallest price `p` with `p - cost >= ceil(75p / 10000)`, which equals `cost + ceil(75 * cost / 9925)`            |
| Markup %          | `markupBpsFromPrice(cost, price) = (9925 * price - 10000 * cost) / cost` in basis points, rounded half up. `null` when cost is 0                |
| Price from markup | `priceFromMarkupBps(cost, k) = max(roundHalfUp(cost * (10000 + k) / 9925), minUnitPriceCents(cost))`. Uncapped. `k = 0` gives the minimum price |

Properties the tests hold the code to:

- `cogs + providerMargin + platformFee === total`, and `total === subtotal`.
- If every line is at or above its minimum price, `providerMargin >= 0`. The order-level fee rounded up is never more than the sum of per-unit fees rounded up, which the minimum price already covers.
- For every cost, `cost + computePlatformFee(minUnitPriceCents(cost)) === minUnitPriceCents(cost)`. The fee the UI shows at the minimum price reconciles with the minimum exactly.
- The result doesn't depend on line order.
- Any value that would exceed `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER`, including the fee's intermediate `subtotal * 75 + 9999`, is rejected as `UNSAFE_AMOUNT` rather than risking a wrong cent.

Why each rule:

- **Integer cents:** floats can't represent cents exactly.
- **Fee on the subtotal, not per line:** computing per line and summing accumulates rounding error.
- **A split conserves the fee** (revision 18.4): `conservedSplitFees(orderFee, paySubtotal)` charges the in-stock part `min(computePlatformFee(paySubtotal), orderFee)` and leaves the rest of the stored fee on the back-order, so the fees along a whole chain add up to the unsplit order's fee. Each part's fee is at most its own rounded-up fee, so neither margin can go negative. The journal uses the order's stored fee, which migration `0015` already checks.
- **Fee rounded up:** the minimum price has to round the per-unit fee up. If it rounded half up, two $0.66 lines priced at $0.66 would produce a $0.01 order fee on $1.32 and a margin of -$0.01. Using the same rounding for the charged fee makes the displayed numbers reconcile (a $5.81 cost has a $5.86 minimum, and the fee shown at $5.86 is $0.05). The platform collects at most one cent more per order than half-up rounding would.
- **Markup uses the exact 0.75% rate,** not the rounded cent fee, so the percentage depends only on price and cost, not on quantity.

The journal functions (`packages/domain/src/money/journal.ts`, revision 16) build and check the postings:

- `paymentJournal(split, { cashAccountId, productSalesAccountId, providerAccountId, providerAccountCode, platformFeeAccountId })` returns `{ accountId?, accountCode, amountCents }` postings in the order Cash (`+total`), product sales (`-cogs`), provider payable (`-margin`), platform fee (`-fee`). Zero amounts are omitted.
- `assertJournalBalances(postings)` throws `LedgerImbalanceError` unless there are at least two non-zero safe-integer postings whose debits and credits are equal.
- `splitFromPostings(postings)` reads `{ cogsCents, providerMarginCents, platformFeeCents, totalCents }` back by account code; it is the inverse of `paymentJournal` for every valid split.

## 6. Core flow

1. **Create a draft.** `POST /api/orders` with a `patientId` the provider owns.
2. **Edit lines.** `PUT /api/orders/:id/lines` replaces all lines. Drafts are lenient: a line may omit the product or the price, and a price may be below the minimum. Save returns 400 for a non-integer, a quantity outside 1–1000, or a price outside 0–1,000,000 cents ($10,000 per unit), and 422 for a duplicate or unknown product. Completeness, stock and the minimum are enforced on send. The builder shows a live split computed with the shared `computeSplit` once all lines are complete.
3. **Send.** `POST /api/orders/:id/send`, in one transaction:
   - lock the order row (`SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`) and require it to be the caller's draft;
   - require at least one line (`EMPTY_ORDER`), every line complete (`INCOMPLETE_LINE`), and every price at or above its minimum (`PRICE_BELOW_MINIMUM`, from `computeSplit`). A $0 line is allowed, but an order total of 0 is 400 `VALIDATION_ERROR` on `lines`, "Order total must be greater than $0.00" (revision 17.2);
   - if any quantity exceeds the product's available stock, require `{ acknowledgeLowStock: true }` in the body, else respond 409 `LOW_STOCK_UNACKNOWLEDGED` listing those lines (revision 14). Stock is not reserved at send;
   - re-snapshot each line's wholesale cost from the product, and store the subtotal, fee and total on the order;
   - set the status to `pending_payment`, record `sentAt`, and add one to `revision`.

   After commit, the notifier is called. A notification failure is logged and doesn't undo the send.

4. **Pay.** `POST /api/orders/:id/pay` by the patient, with the body `{ expectedRevision, expectedTotalCents, mode?, expectedPayableLines?, simulateFailure? }`: the revision and total the patient's screen showed. `mode` is `full` (the default) or `available` (revision 14, below). Pay runs as several short transactions, and the gateway is called between them, outside any lock:
   1. **Prepare** (one transaction, order row locked `FOR UPDATE`). If the order was never sent, respond 404; if it's already `paid`, return the existing result without charging again, provided the request names the paid revision and total; if it's otherwise not `pending_payment`, respond 409 `ORDER_NOT_PAYABLE`. A back-order with no attempt in progress whose parent isn't paid is 409 `PRIOR_ORDER_UNPAID` (revision 15.3). If the order's current `revision` or `totalCents` differs from what the request names, respond 409 `ORDER_CHANGED` and charge nothing. An unresolved attempt left over from an earlier revision, or a `late_success` anywhere on the order, is a 409 `PAYMENT_NEEDS_RECONCILIATION`. Then pick the attempt to charge: an unresolved attempt for this revision (`pending`, `succeeded` without a payment, or `conflict`) is reused with its existing key. An `abandoned` latest attempt for this revision is re-run with its existing key (revision 19.1): its stock is taken again if every line is still in stock, and it becomes `pending`; if stock ran out, its quote is `payable: false` (`awaiting_restock`) and pay is 409 `INSUFFICIENT_STOCK`, because splitting would need a new key. Only when there's no attempt yet, or the latest was `declined` (or `refund_required`), does a new execution start: the order's product rows are locked in ascending id (so two orders can't deadlock) and planned with `planPartialPayment`. A `full` pay with any shortfall is a 409 `INSUFFICIENT_STOCK` carrying per-line `availability` and the current `partialPaymentQuote`, before any charge. A payable total of 0 (a legacy zero-total order, or an `available` plan whose in-stock lines cost nothing) is 409 `ORDER_CHANGED` with no quote, before any split, reservation or charge (revision 17.2). Then stock for every line is decremented with a conditional update (`stock >= quantity`, same order), and a `pending` attempt with key `<installId>-order-<id>-r<revision>-e<execution>` and `stockHeld` is inserted (`installId` is the database's own UUID, migration 0016; attempts made before revision 18 keep their unprefixed keys). This transaction commits before the gateway is called.

      **When a new execution, and so a new key, may start** (revision 19.1): only after the gateway definitively declined the previous one, never after an abandonment or a timeout. At the same revision an abandoned execution is re-run with its key, as above. For a new revision (the order was cancelled, reopened and re-sent), every `abandoned` attempt from an earlier revision is first looked up at the gateway, outside the lock and never charging: a success found there is recorded on that attempt as `late_success` and the pay is 409 `PAYMENT_NEEDS_RECONCILIATION` with no new key; a decline marks it `declined`; nothing found lets the new revision get its first key. Prepare re-checks under the lock that every such attempt was looked up.
   - **Partial pay (`mode: 'available'`, revision 14).** `expectedTotalCents` is the in-stock total the patient saw (`partialPaymentQuote.pay.totalCents`), and `expectedPayableLines` (required, revision 15.2) is exactly the in-stock lines the patient saw, as `{ productId, quantity }` in any order with zero-quantity lines left out. In the same prepare transaction, after the lock and the reconciliation gate: nothing in stock is 409 `AWAITING_STOCK`; a stale revision, in-stock total or set of payable lines is 409 `ORDER_CHANGED` with the fresh `partialPaymentQuote`, so an equal-total swap or redistribution of what's in stock is never charged without a fresh approval; no shortfall continues exactly as `full`. With a shortfall, one back-order is inserted (`pending_payment`, sent now, revision 1, same patient and provider, the short quantities at the original unit prices and cost snapshots, `splitFromOrderId`/`splitFromRevision` set), this order's lines are reduced to the in-stock quantities (lines at zero are deleted), its totals and fee are re-snapshotted, its revision goes up by one, and the reservation and attempt are made at the new revision. A retry of the same request after the split committed (its back-order came from `expectedRevision`, the current revision is one higher, and the current total equals `expectedTotalCents`) is treated as a pay of the current revision, so it never splits twice; this also holds after a restart and for concurrent requests, which serialize on the order lock. A decline after a split leaves the split in place. The split charges the in-stock part `ceil(75 × paySubtotal / 10000)`, capped at the order's stored fee, and stores the rest of the stored fee on the back-order (revision 18.4), so the fees across a chain always add up to the unsplit order's fee. An order that already has a live back-order (cancelled ones don't count, revision 18.1) and is short again can only be paid once restocked: its quote is `payable: false` with `reason: 'awaiting_restock'`, and a pay is 409 (`INSUFFICIENT_STOCK`, or `AWAITING_STOCK` when nothing is in stock). The response is the paid order, with `backorderOrderId` and its new `revision`.
   2. **Charge** the `PaymentGateway` with the attempt's key and amount. An attempt that already `succeeded` isn't charged again: its outcome is checked with the gateway's `lookup`, which never charges, and must show a success for the same amount and reference.
   3. **Apply the outcome** (one transaction, order locked), fenced: the attempt is updated only if it's still the same execution and still `pending` or `conflict` (or `abandoned`, for a success or a decline), so a stale or duplicate response changes nothing. A success stores the gateway reference. A success for an execution that is no longer current (it was `abandoned`, or its order is no longer awaiting payment at its revision) is recorded as `late_success` with its reference, releases any stock it held, and pay returns 409 `PAYMENT_NEEDS_RECONCILIATION`; it stays in the admin's unresolved list until reconciled (revision 19.1). A decline marks the attempt `declined`, releases its stock in the same transaction (only if the fenced update matched) and returns 402 `PAYMENT_FAILED`. An `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED` refusal, or a success for a different amount, isn't a decline: the attempt becomes `conflict`, keeps its stock, and pay returns 409 `PAYMENT_NEEDS_RECONCILIATION`.
   4. **Finalize** (one transaction, order locked): check that the order is still `pending_payment` at the attempt's revision and total, recompute the split from the snapshotted lines with `computeSplit` and abort if it doesn't reproduce the stored total, insert the `Payment` pointing at the attempt (its held stock becomes the sale), build the payment's journal with `paymentJournal`, assert it balances and debits Cash with exactly the payment amount, insert the journal and its postings, and mark the order `paid` with `paidAt`.

   If the gateway call itself throws (a transport error or a timeout, revision 18.2), the outcome is unknown: the attempt stays `pending` with its stock held, and pay answers 503 `PAYMENT_PENDING` with `{ orderId, revision, amountCents }`. A retried pay charges the same key, which replays the gateway's outcome, or an admin reconciles it (below).

   If finalizing fails, it rolls back alone: the attempt stays `succeeded` with no payment and keeps its stock, and the request fails with a 500. A retried pay with the same revision and total verifies that success by lookup and finalizes it, with no second charge. Concurrent pays of one attempt send the same key, and the gateway gives them the identical outcome. The unique constraints on `payments.order_id`, `payments.payment_attempt_id` and `journals.payment_id`, and the deferred journal triggers, back this at the database level.

   **Blocking.** While any attempt for the order is `pending`, `succeeded` without a payment, `conflict` or `late_success`, cancel, reopen, delete and line edits return 409 `PAYMENT_NEEDS_RECONCILIATION`, ahead of any status error. A `declined`, `abandoned` or `refund_required` latest attempt, or a recorded payment, unblocks the order.

   **Recovery in the UI** (revision 15.1). While an attempt is unresolved, the order DTOs carry `paymentInProgress: { revision, amountCents }`, the quote and line availability count the attempt's reservation as the order's own stock (so the order stays payable even if it took the last units), and the patient page shows "Your payment is being processed" with a **Complete payment** button that pays again with exactly that revision and amount. That retry reuses the attempt's key, so it can't start a new charge.

   **Recovery after a restart.** Attempts and the fake gateway's charges are both in the database, so nothing depends on process memory. A `pending` attempt left by a crash is resolved by the next pay: charging its key again replays the stored outcome if the gateway saw the first call, or makes the one charge if it never did. A `succeeded` attempt without a payment is finalized after a lookup, as above. Nothing resolves an attempt on its own: there's no background job.

   **Admin reconcile** (revision 18.2). `GET /api/admin/payment-attempts?status=unresolved` lists attempts that are `pending`, `succeeded` without a payment, `conflict` or `late_success`, plus `refund_required` ones not yet refunded (revision 19.1); each row has `gatewayReference` and `refundedAt`. `POST /api/admin/payment-attempts/:id/reconcile` settles one from the gateway's `lookup`, which never charges. A success the gateway holds is finalized exactly as a retried pay would (payment, journal, paid; no new charge). A decline marks the attempt `declined` and releases its stock under the usual fence. No record at all, once the attempt is at least 15 minutes old on the app's clock (injectable for tests), marks it `abandoned` and releases its stock, but retires no key: the next pay re-runs it with the same key (above). No record before that is 409 `RECONCILE_TOO_EARLY`. Reconciling a resolved attempt answers `outcome: 'unchanged'`. Releases are recorded in `inventory_adjustments` as `reconcile_release`, and the attempt records `reconciledAt` and the admin. A conflict whose gateway record is for another amount still needs manual reconciliation (see §14).

   **Late successes** (revision 19.1). Reconciling a `late_success` (or an abandoned attempt whose lookup now finds a success) checks the gateway's record, then, in one transaction under the order lock: if the order still awaits payment at the attempt's revision and total, no other execution is unresolved, and every line is in stock, the attempt becomes `succeeded` and its payment and journal are recorded (`outcome: 'finalized'`, stock taken now). Otherwise (the order was paid by another execution, cancelled or changed, or sold out) the attempt becomes `refund_required` (`outcome: 'refund_required'`): the patient was charged for a quote the order can no longer honor. The admin panel shows it as "Charged twice: refund $X", and `POST /api/admin/payment-attempts/:id/refund` calls the gateway's `refund(key)` (a stub in the fake that records `refunded_at` on the stored charge; real refunds are out of scope) and sets the attempt's `refundedAt` (`outcome: 'refunded'`; again, `'unchanged'`; any other status, 409 `NOT_REFUNDABLE`).

   **What is guaranteed.** One key is charged at most once, so a retry, a restart, a timeout or an abandonment followed by a retry at the same revision can never produce a second charge. A second charge is possible only when a call delayed beyond the 15-minute window lands after the order was re-sent and the lookup had found nothing; it is then always recorded (`late_success`, then `refund_required`) and listed for the admin until refunded, never silent.

5. **Cancel.** `POST /api/orders/:id/cancel` by the provider, for `pending_payment` orders only. It cancels the order's downstream back-orders too (revision 15.3): after locking the order it locks the rest of the chain in ascending id, and if any of them has an unresolved attempt the whole cancel is 409 `PAYMENT_NEEDS_RECONCILIATION` and nothing changes. The detail DTOs carry `backorderStatus`, which the provider page uses for the note "Back-order #B was cancelled with this order" on a cancelled original. It takes the same row lock as pay's transactions, so a cancel and a pay can't both succeed: a cancel that commits first makes the pay a 409 `ORDER_NOT_PAYABLE`, and once pay has committed an attempt, cancel gets 409 `PAYMENT_NEEDS_RECONCILIATION` until the attempt is declined, or `ORDER_NOT_CANCELLABLE` once the order is paid. Cancelling writes no journal and doesn't touch stock; a declined attempt has already given its stock back. The patient is notified and sees the order as cancelled, with no Pay button.
6. **Delete a draft.** `DELETE /api/orders/:id` locks the order, requires the caller's draft (409 `ORDER_NOT_DRAFT` otherwise), locks its downstream back-orders, deletes their and its lines, any declined attempts left from before a reopen, and the orders, and returns 204. If a send wins the lock first, the delete gets the 409; if the delete wins, the send gets a 404.
7. **Reopen.** `POST /api/orders/:id/reopen` locks the order, rejects a back-order (409 `BACKORDER_NOT_EDITABLE`), requires it to be `cancelled` (409 `ORDER_NOT_REOPENABLE` otherwise), and returns it to `draft` as described in §4. Stock is untouched, since only pay attempts move stock. The revision is kept, so the next send is a new quote.
8. **Read.** Orders are read from the `orders_with_status` view, so `status` may be the derived `awaiting_stock`. Both order DTOs carry `splitFromOrderId` and `backorderOrderId`, and, while the order awaits payment or stock, a `partialPaymentQuote` (`pay` and `backorder` totals, with the order's stored fee conserved across the two halves as in the split above, plus `payable` and `hasShortfall`; the platform fee appears only in the provider's copy) and each line's live `availableQuantity`, `payableQuantity` and `shortQuantity`. The provider's order detail shows the snapshotted lines, the split, the payment reference and the payment's `journal` (`{ id, postedAt, memo, postings: [{ accountCode, accountName, debitCents, creditCents }] }`, null until paid). The patient's view has its own DTO type with no cost, margin, fee, journal or posting fields, so those can't leak by accident.

## 8. Metrics

- **GMV** is the sum of the Cash debits on the provider's payment journals: what patients actually paid.
- **Margin earned** is the sum of the credits to the provider's payable account on those journals, and **processing fees** the sum of their Platform fee revenue credits.
- **Owed to you** (`owedToYouCents`, revision 16) is the provider's payable account balance, all time and never date-filtered. With no payouts yet it equals all-time margin earned.

The dashboard tiles read the postings rather than recomputing anything, all four in one SQL statement so they describe one snapshot (with "Sold by product", revision 18.3: units and the sum of line totals per product over the provider's orders paid in range, by revenue descending, in the same statement), and an aggregate beyond `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` is a 500 rather than a rounded number. Cancelled and unpaid orders contribute nothing because they have no payment or journal. GMV counts finalized payments: a charge that succeeded at the gateway but hasn't been finalized yet is missing until a retried pay records it. The API takes `range` (`month` by default, `last30`, `all` or `custom`), `from`/`to` calendar days (inclusive, `custom` only, at most 366 days) and `tz` (an IANA zone, UTC by default). The server resolves the range to days relative to today in `tz` (`month` is the 1st through today, `last30` is today and the 29 days before) and filters the three ranged totals on `orders.paid_at` (via the journal's order) inside that same statement, over `[local midnight of the first day, local midnight of the day after the last)`, which Postgres computes so a 23- or 25-hour DST day is exact. The response echoes the resolved `range` (`preset`, `from`, `to`, `timeZone`; days are null for `all`) so the client only displays it. Invalid input is a 400 `VALIDATION_ERROR` naming the field. Inventory is not date-filtered. Platform-wide fee revenue is the `4100` balance in the admin trial balance.

## 10. Search and pagination

- Products and patients use offset pagination returning `{ items, total, offset, limit }`. The default limit is 25 and the maximum 100. Ordering is by name, then id, so pages are stable.
- Search is a case-insensitive substring match (`ILIKE '%q%'`) with `%`, `_` and `\` escaped, so they match literally. Products match name or SKU. Patients match name, plus an exact date of birth when the whole query is a valid date. Address is deliberately not searched.
- A btree index can't serve a substring match, so migration `0005` enables `pg_trgm` and adds GIN trigram indexes on `products.name`, `products.sku` and `users.name`. The queries `ILIKE` the bare columns, which is what those indexes match.
- `npm run explain:search -w apps/api` loads 50,000 products and 20,000 patients into a throwaway database and prints the query plans before and after the trigram indexes. In one local run, a selective product name search went from about 38 ms (sequential scan) to about 0.2 ms (bitmap index scan on the trigram index), and the matching count query from about 40 ms to about 0.1 ms. A common term such as "ashwagandha" doesn't benefit on the first page, because a page of 25 is found quickly by walking the name index. Its count query still dropped from about 44 ms to about 5 ms. Timings vary by machine.
- The provider order list has its own indexes. Migration `0004` adds `(provider_id, status, sent_at, id)` for the oldest-sent-first awaiting-payment view. Migration `0006` adds two indexes on `(provider_id, [status,] coalesce(paid_at, cancelled_at, sent_at, created_at) desc, id desc)`, matching the "most recent activity" sort, plus `(patient_id, created_at desc, id desc)` for a patient's orders.

## 11. Front end

- Vue 3 single-page app with Vue Router (history mode) and PrimeVue 4 (Aura preset, light only). Lazy `DataTable`s map directly onto the API's `{ items, total, offset, limit }` pages. Money and percent inputs are plain text inputs parsed by the shared integer parser, never PrimeVue's float-based `InputNumber`.
- Provider navigation is **Patients | Orders | Dashboard**; a patient sees **My Orders**; an admin sees **Inventory | Accounts** (stock, then the trial balance and journal list, where each journal shows "Order #N" as plain text and its provider). The routes guard on the role from the server's user record and redirect the other persona with a notice. That's a convenience, not security; the API enforces access.
- **Orders tab:** a paginated table of the provider's orders with a multi-select Status filter (Draft, Awaiting payment, Awaiting stock, Paid, Cancelled, plus a select-all box). The default is all statuses. An empty selection also means all statuses, shown with the placeholder "All statuses".
- **Order detail actions** depend on status: Delete draft, Cancel order (awaiting payment) and Reopen as draft (cancelled), each behind a confirmation dialog. Reopen takes the provider straight to the builder. Cancelling an original also cancels its back-orders, and the cancelled original notes "Back-order #B was cancelled with this order."
- **Patient order page:** while a payment attempt is unresolved it shows "Your payment is being processed" with **Complete payment** (revision 15.1); a back-order whose earlier order is unpaid hides Pay and shows "Pay order #A first" with a link (revision 15.3).
- **Dashboard:** a date-range control (This month by default, Last 30 days, All time, or a custom range; the server resolves the days in the browser's time zone), three tiles (GMV, Your margin earned, Processing fees), links "See paid orders →" (`/orders?status=paid`) and "See orders awaiting payment →" (`/orders?status=pending_payment`), and a searchable, read-only inventory table.
- **URL state:** every list's filters, search and page live in query parameters: Orders `status` and `page`, Patients `q`, `status` and `page`, and the inventory's `q` and `page`. Defaults are left out of the URL. Changing a filter resets the page. Typing in a search box updates the URL with `router.replace` (debounced), so it doesn't flood history; filter and page changes use `router.push`, so Back steps through them. A shared `useListUrl` helper, used by Orders, Patients and the Dashboard, guards these writes: navigating to another path (or unmounting) cancels any pending debounced search, `replace` writes happen only while the view's route is still current and no navigation is in flight, and `push` writes only while the router is still on the view. Without that guard, a debounced `router.replace` could land after a link click had started navigating (while the lazy target view loaded) and abort it.
- **Layout:** page content sits in a centered column with a 1200px max width. The header bar is full width with its contents aligned to that column.
- Views fetch through one typed function per endpoint in `apps/web/src/api/`, with no client cache. State is composables and `ref`/`computed`; there's no store library.

## 12. Testing strategy

- **Domain (Vitest):** reference-value tables for the fee, minimum price and markup conversions, validation order and error codes, overflow boundaries, a 1,000-order randomized sweep checking the sum identities and margin `>= 0`, and money parsing (no floating-point multiplication).
- **API (Vitest + Supertest, real Postgres):** black-box HTTP contract tests written only from the DTOs and the endpoint list, plus implementation-side tests for pay rollback after the journal insert, the double-entry database guards, the real `0014` backfill (a scratch database migrated to `0013`, given pre-revision-16 payments and ledger rows, then migrated to `0014`), migration `0015` over existing data (app-made payments including a back-order pass; a payment with no attempt fails the migration and leaves the database at `0014`), migration `0017` the same way (a payment whose reference differs from its attempt fails it), revision 19 abandonment safety (the auditor's interleaving: a held first call, 16 minutes, abandon, retry, release, ending in one charge and one payment; late successes finalized or marked `refund_required` and refunded; no new revision key until the lookup clears the abandoned one) and forged-provenance inserts rejected at commit, zero-total send and pay, idempotent double pay, stale quotes, concurrent pay and cancel pairs, payment execution safety (held and overlapping gateway responses, fenced outcomes, a key conflict that keeps its reservation, last-unit competition, and restarts simulated with a freshly constructed gateway and app on the same database, checking that the gateway's successful references equal the recorded payments), delete and reopen, the order list filters and sort orders, ownership and persona rules, search and pagination, and production SPA serving. Each checkout uses its own test database, truncated and reseeded with stable ids per test.
- **Web (Vitest + Vue Testing Library + jsdom):** behavior tests for the builder (linked Markup % and price, minimum-price state, lenient drafts), pay, login and lists; loading, error, empty and forbidden-persona states for every data-loading view; and axe-core scans of each screen and key state, including PrimeVue's teleported dialog. Testing Library conventions are enforced by lint.
- **End to end (Playwright, Chromium, also in CI since revision 19.4):** twelve spec files (28 tests) against the real API and Vite on a dedicated `supplement_orders_e2e` database, reset once before each run. The happy path checks the processing fee shown, the cost + fee = minimum identity, the journal's split, and that the dashboard GMV, margin and fees rise by exactly the order's amounts while stock drops by exactly the quantity. The others cover a simulated gateway failure, cancelling, the order lifecycle (delete a draft, send, cancel, reopen, pay, and the Orders and Patients status filters), a stale patient page that gets "order changed" and pays the new total after reloading, and URL-state navigation: four cases that type into a debounced search and then click a link while the next view's code is still loading, which reproduces the bug described in §11.
- **Cross-model test authoring:** most suites were written first by a different model (Grok) from the one implementing (Claude), against written contracts. Codex finished the revision 10 payment tests. See [AI_USAGE.md](AI_USAGE.md).

## 13. Deployment

The app is deployed at <https://web-production-b7c8c.up.railway.app> from the GitLab repository: GitLab CI deploys every push to `main`, so the live site runs the code described here. The README's [Deployment](../README.md#deployment) section has the exact commands and variables.

- **Railway project** `supplement-orders` with two services: `web` (the app) and `Postgres`. `DATABASE_URL` on `web` references the Postgres service's private URL.
- **Build:** Railpack with Node 22 (`engines` is `>=22.13 <23`; `.nvmrc` is `22`, revision 18.4), running `npm run build`. Railpack's install step already includes dev dependencies such as Vite.
- **Pre-deploy:** `npm run db:migrate && npm run db:seed:if-empty`. The seed step loads the full demo seed (the named users, 12 small-stock products and 250 patients) only when the database has no users, and never deletes anything.
- **Run:** `npm start` runs the API under `tsx`. With `NODE_ENV=production` the same process serves the built SPA from `apps/web/dist`: fingerprinted `/assets/` files are cached for a year, a missing `/assets/` file is a plain 404 rather than `index.html`, `index.html` is `no-cache`, and any other non-API GET falls back to `index.html`, so a route such as `/orders?status=paid` works. Unknown `/api` paths still return a JSON 404. One origin, no CORS. The process refuses to start if the web build is missing.
- **Health check:** `GET /api/health`, with restart on failure.
- `NODE_ENV=production` also disables `simulateFailure`, so the decline path can only be demonstrated locally, by sending that field on the pay request.
- **Config:** `railway.json` records the build and deploy settings, but Railway has [deprecated Config as Code](https://docs.railway.com/infrastructure-as-code#migrating-from-config-as-code) and new services don't read it. The same settings were applied to the service directly. If the service is ever recreated, they have to be applied again.
- **Deploys** run from GitLab CI: the `deploy` job in `.gitlab-ci.yml` runs on every push to `main` and calls `railway up --ci --service web` with `RAILWAY_TOKEN`, a Railway project token stored as a masked CI/CD variable. (Railway's repo auto-deploy supports only GitHub.) Since revision 18.4, `deploy` needs the `test` job, which runs on every pipeline on the same `railway-deploy` runner in `node:22` with a `postgres:16` service: `npm ci`, lint, typecheck, `npm test` and build. Since revision 19.4 it also needs the `e2e` job, which runs in parallel with `test` on the same runner, in the official Playwright image matching `@playwright/test` (`mcr.microsoft.com/playwright:v1.63.0-noble`, browsers preinstalled) with a `postgres:16` service: `npm ci`, then `npm run test:e2e` (one worker, 2 retries in CI, a 20-minute job timeout, traces kept on failure). The image tag moves with the Playwright version.
- **Accepted risk:** auth is stubbed on the public URL too, so anyone can act as any seeded user. All data is fake.

## 14. Production hardening and future work

- **Refunds** as new reversing journals, never edits to existing ones. Cancelling a paid order is out of scope today.
- **Provider payouts:** each provider's payable balance says what it is owed; a payout would be a journal debiting the payable and crediting Cash, and actually moving money is a separate system.
- **Payment reconciliation:** revision 18.2 adds the admin's reconcile, driven by the gateway's lookup. A real integration would run it as a scheduled job, use the processor's webhooks, and void the old key at the processor before abandoning an attempt. Revision 19.1 keeps an abandoned key alive instead (a retry at the same revision re-runs it, so it charges at most once) and records any late success, but a call delayed past the window can still charge after the order was re-sent at a new total; that charge is listed as `refund_required` for a person to refund. A `conflict` for another amount still needs a person. The fake gateway shares the app's database for convenience; a real processor's store is its own.
- **Real auth and RBAC:** replace `HeaderAuth` with real authentication behind the same interface. Date of birth and address are PHI, so access to them needs HIPAA-grade access controls and audit logging.
- **Rate limiting** on the API, especially pay and search.
- **Observability:** structured logs with order and payment ids, metrics on pay latency and gateway declines, and alerting on any `LedgerImbalanceError` or post-charge rollback.
- **Cleanup:** the old `lower(name)` btree indexes are unused and could be dropped.
